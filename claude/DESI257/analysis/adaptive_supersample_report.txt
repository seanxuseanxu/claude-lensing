Module-stated limitations (adaptive_supersample.py docstring, NOT re-derived here):
  - EXPERIMENTAL: 'moves faster than the core scene API; treat its interface as less stable' (how-to admonition).
  - PSF convention: bin-first (scatter to native grid, convolve with the native kernel) -- NOT the subgrid_kernel supersampled convolution the stock ss>=2 pipeline uses. The two conventions can differ by several sigma at a cuspy peak (module docstring: ~4 sigma on the bundled demo); this project's own uniform ladder independently measured a 3.75 sigma convention gap on DESI257 at ss_ref=64. Reported above as the 'subgrid' psf_mode delta, which conflates this gap with the factor map's own quadrature error and is NOT reducible by refining the grid.
  - The factor map is frozen from the DATA at construction, so it is only accurate for models whose flux stays within `dilate` native pixels of where the data shows flux. Early burn-in proposals displaced further than that, or model features (e.g. a predicted faint counter-image) the data does not show at all, are rendered at the coarse tier and their likelihood/gradient carries the corresponding quadrature error -- compare_to_reference only certifies the exact params tested.
  - DEFAULT_SNR_LEVELS are 'STARTING POINTS calibrated on the bundled demo system'; the module explicitly says to re-derive per-system with compare_to_reference, which is what the setting sweep above does.
  - The map is static (frozen, not model-derived) specifically to keep the likelihood's shape fixed under jit and smooth in the parameters -- a real constraint on any HMC use, not just a performance choice.
